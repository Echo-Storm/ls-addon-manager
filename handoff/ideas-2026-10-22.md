# Where to improve next (2026-10-22), after the Reddit feedback and a look at other DLSS projects

What the feedback says, in one line each: EchoFG flickers (grain, menus, fast motion at 30 fps); the upscalers cost a lot, most on a weak second card or an RTX 20/30; video memory is high; Lossless Scaling's counter was wrong with EchoFG; people want presets, a FAQ and less fiddling.

## Done today (all unreleased, see the CHANGELOG)

- Lossless Scaling's counter shows EchoFG's rate; EchoFG warns when Lossless Scaling's own frame generation is on too.
- EchoFG: grain matching (noise level pulse between real and made frames, measured 70 % -> 81 % of the real frames' detail on a noisy clip), menu/text changes show one real frame or the other (12.7 -> 3.3 levels from the nearer menu).
- Upscalers: the lighter upscaling of generated frames is on by default (it was an off-by-default test: the per-presented-frame cost was the big one); it also starts when the upscaler alone takes over 55 % of the card; a Preset (Quality / Balanced / Performance); the video memory in the status line; DLSS Auto picks the light model K on RTX 20/30/GTX 16 (L costs those cards up to 3x).
- Tools: `collect_test_logs.ps1`, `nr_fgeval` flicker/detail/noise/grain/menu checks.

## Ideas, by what they would buy

1. **Latency (asked twice on Reddit).** EchoFG shows each real frame one frame late (it needs the next one). Measure it (nr_fgpresent has the presents' timing) and say it plainly in the panel; check whether Reflex-like pacing (present the real frame at once and fill the gap after it) can cut part of it. OptiScaler (GPL-3.0: ideas only, no code) hooks Reflex/Anti-Lag through a stand-in library; we cannot, we are after the game.
2. **Hardware optical flow on NVIDIA (RTX 20 and newer).** The Optical Flow SDK (D3D12, the engine is in the driver) gives the motion field DLSS frame generation uses, for almost no shader time. EchoFG's flow is shader work, ~1 ms at 1440p half size, and its failures (fast motion, repeating patterns) are flow failures. The SDK's licence allows shipping the distributable parts inside an application with more functionality; it needs a download of the SDK (the owner's yes) and a fallback to our own flow for other cards. Worth a prototype and a measurement against `nr_fgeval`.
3. **Per-stage cost table in the panel** (upscale, motion, lean, sharpen, generated-frame run, in ms and as a share of the card), so a reporter's screenshot says where the time goes. The pieces are already measured (`GpuMs`, `MotionMs`).
4. **Video memory.** Alias the intermediate textures (smoothed into unsharpened/leaned): one full-size texture less, 30 MB at 1440p HDR, 66 MB at 4K HDR. The rest is the vendors' (DLSS +282 MB, FSR +204, XeSS +184 at 1440p SDR, measured).
5. **Grain matching for HDR** (SDR only today); a per-region noise map for mixed scenes.
6. **A real "which GPU does what"** (board F1): the upscaler or the model on the render card, frame generation on the second. Needs cross-adapter copies; the plumbing for the model is proven in tools, nothing is built.
7. **Wine/Proton:** nobody runs Lossless Scaling's addons there; skip, say so in the FAQ.
8. **An honest FAQ** (board D1): anti-cheat (the manager is a library Lossless Scaling loads and hooks only inside that process, never the game), VirusTotal flags (hooking libraries look like injectors to scanners), older cards, what runs on which GPU, motion vectors.

## What the other projects teach

- **OptiScaler** (GPL-3.0): the swap-the-upscaler idea in the game itself; its features worth matching in spirit: RCAS-style sharpening and output scaling (we have our own sharpening), DLSS preset overrides (we have them), a frame generation that needs game data (we cannot). Not usable as code.
- **The DLSS 5 / Neural Rendering mods (RenoDX, the Feeder tools)** report the network costs about half the frame rate; they also describe running it after the upscaler (stable) or before (cheaper, experimental), the question board #23 measured as a tie here.
- **Streamline / NGX:** DLSS 4.5's L and M models are heavier on cards without FP8 (NVIDIA's own words); this is why Auto is now K there.
- **lsfg-vk and MFG Super Motion:** studied before (docs); nothing new.

Sources for the web part: NVIDIA's Optical Flow SDK 5.0 licence and programming guides (docs.nvidia.com/video-technologies/optical-flow-sdk), the OptiScaler repository, TechSpot and Notebookcheck / PCGH DLSS 4.5 benchmarks.
